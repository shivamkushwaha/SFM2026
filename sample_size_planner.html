What the curve is telling you.

  • The curve rises as the inverse square of E. Halving the tolerance multiplies the required sample by four, which is a fourfold rise in field cost for a twofold gain in precision.
  • For a proportion, leave p at 0.50 unless a pilot study gives you something better. That is the worst case, so the resulting n is guaranteed to be enough.
  • Try the classic settings: 95 percent confidence with a tolerance of 3 percentage points gives 1068, which is why national opinion polls settle near a thousand respondents.
  • Always round up. A calculated 43.30 means 44, because 43 would leave the margin slightly wider than the one promised.
