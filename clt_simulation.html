How to run this in class. Start with the exponential population at n = 1 and draw 1000. The right panel is simply the population again, because a sample of one is the observation itself. Now move n to 2, then 5, then 30, drawing 1000 each time.

Watch the two skewness figures. The population skewness never moves. The skewness of the sample means falls towards zero roughly as one divided by √n, which is the theorem doing its work. The heavy tailed option shows why the rule of n at least 30 is a convention rather than a guarantee.
