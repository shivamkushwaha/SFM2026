The same argument as for the mean, applied to the statistic that opinion polls and quality audits actually report.

Distribution of p̂ across samples

Each bar counts samples, not people. The curve is the normal approximation with centre p and spread √[ p(1 − p) divided by n ].

Three things to demonstrate.

  • Hold n at 200 and sweep p from 0.50 towards 0.95. The spread narrows, because p(1 − p) is largest at 0.50. Polling a split electorate is the hardest case.
  • Hold p at 0.50 and quadruple n from 50 to 200. The standard error halves, exactly as it does for a mean.
  • Set p to 0.02 with n at 40. The condition fails, the histogram piles against zero, and the normal curve visibly runs past the boundary into impossible territory. That is when the binomial must be used instead.
